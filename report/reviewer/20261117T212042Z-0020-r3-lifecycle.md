## What the correction closes

`JobContext.scope()` now calls `guard_commit()` under a real transaction;
`guard_commit()` locks the current job row until commit and checks token/epoch
and current lease validity (`jobs/engine.py:220–270`). Network service units
receive and call that guard at their actual commit boundary
(`network/services.py:1270–1342,1377–1442`, `network/handlers.py:89–91,139`).
This resolves the specific check-to-commit race described by R2 REV-1711-01:
a concurrent owner transfer either wins first and causes rollback, or waits
behind the short unit's row lock. This is materially different from another
plain ownership SELECT. I/O remains outside these domain transactions in the
reviewed paths. Overall A01/A05 closure is nevertheless incomplete below.

The post-acquisition sender hold (`jobs/engine.py:783–813`) now includes other
EXECUTING/VERIFYING jobs even with valid leases. It closes the particular R2
hold-to-acquire race and the direct different-job successor after predecessor DB
session loss. This is a useful correction, but recovery can still remove the
same job's unknown hold without proving its old adapter stopped.

The watchdog (`jobs/engine.py:590–629`) genuinely uses HEARTBEAT_S and rejects
an expired lease in its own conditional UPDATE. The submitted tests prove
actual renewal and watchdog-specific non-resurrection, as opposed to merely
declaring a heartbeat constant. Other renewal paths do not share its predicate.

## Residual findings

### REV-1711-04 — P1 PARTIAL: same-job recovery still trusts released DB lock

**Source:** `jobs/engine.py:1145–1176`, especially the sole old-writer check at
`1154` (`acquire_router_sender`) before `resolve_uncertain(applied=False)` sets
RETRY_WAIT. The newer hold at `792–799` excludes the job itself.

**Reachable schedule:** engine worker is blocked in adapter execution after a
send; its own PostgreSQL session is terminated and the advisory lock really
releases, while its adapter thread remains live. Lease is expired/reconciled to
UNCERTAIN. A negative readback at that instant says not applied *yet*, not that
the outstanding old command cannot land later. `resolve_uncertain(...,
applied=False)` acquires the released lock and changes that same row to
RETRY_WAIT. Claim bumps its epoch, but the new worker excludes its own ID from
the durable unknown hold and sends again while the old adapter is still live.
The old worker's eventual DB OperationalError cannot retract that old send.

This is the existing REV04 old-writer lifecycle requirement, not a new scope or
an invented public endpoint. The packet explicitly includes resolve_uncertain
and forbids treating DB ownership as a way to retract RouterOS commands.

**Minimum correction:** keep the unknown hold until independent, applicable
old-adapter/process safety evidence and readback make retry safe. Acquiring a
fresh PostgreSQL session lock alone is insufficient. Apply the same invariant
to same-job recovery/reclaim as to a different successor, without introducing a
queue/service/framework. Recovery must still allow a genuinely stopped writer
and safe not-applied readback to progress; other routers remain independent.

**Closure probe:**
`test_readback_not_applied_cannot_resume_after_db_loss_with_live_old_writer`.
Only its own disposable runtime DB session is terminated; real engine,
reconcile, resolve and claim paths run. Events bound the old adapter; joins and
all worker outcomes are collected. Expected one send, no retry reopening while
the old adapter can still deliver. The collected old OperationalError is an
expected fault outcome, not an unhandled thread/harness exception.

### REV-1711-01 / REV-1711-09 — P1 residual: non-watchdog paths revive expired leases

**Source:** `JobContext._fenced/heartbeat/progress` at
`jobs/engine.py:145–193` has token/epoch/nonterminal predicates but no current
lease-validity predicate. `_fenced_update` at `412–420` also omits validity.
The engine uses those paths at `817–825` and `847–854` before executing or
verifying, and terminal success CAS at `906–913` also lacks expiry.

**Reachable schedule:** while a single execute phase is blocked, expire its
lease; the real watchdog observes expiry and exits without renewing. Before a
reconciler happens to run, let the handler resume. Its next `ctx.progress()`
silently renews the expired lease and allows another send; alternatively just
returning from execute causes the engine to transition VERIFYING and call
`ctx.heartbeat()`, reviving the expired lease and committing SUCCEEDED.
The submitted non-resurrection test always reconciles before releasing its
worker (`test_jobs_long_phase_0020_r2.py:206–209`), hiding both schedules.

The production backup handler uses this exact `ctx.progress()` boundary before
`adapter.create_backup` (`backups/service.py:191–204`) and after network I/O
before its storage write (`236–237`). The controlled probes use a test kind to
isolate the actual engine/renewal behavior without contacting a device.

**Minimum correction:** every worker renewal, state/result/domain mutation and
effect-boundary check must require a currently valid lease in the applicable
expected state, not merely the same token/epoch. Loss/expiry must stop the old
worker instead of waiting for a reconciler to remove its token. Preserve useful
watchdog liveness, locked commit guard, CAS transfer/terminal behavior and honest
uncertain recovery. No schema/framework expansion is presumed necessary.

**Closure probes:**
`test_progress_cannot_revive_expired_worker_after_watchdog_stopped` and
`test_returning_io_worker_cannot_revive_expired_lease_before_verify`.
They gate real watchdog completion and engine execution using independent
connections. Expected zero new send/verify under the expired ownership, no
lease resurrection and no false terminal success, even without an immediate
reconciler. Root may group this under the existing REV01/09 closure rather than
create duplicate findings for the common predicate defect.
